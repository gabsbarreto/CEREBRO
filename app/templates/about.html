ABOUT CEREBRO

CEREBRO

A workspace for extracting research data from articles and spreadsheets.

PDF extraction Apply a prompt to full-text PDFs and review each result.

Use a flexible prompt to produce free-text or Markdown answers from full-text articles. This is useful when the information varies between papers or needs explanation rather than fixed fields.

Example: Summarize how each study describes its intervention, results, and limitations, including details found in different sections of the article.

Process PDFs using local OCR with a local or OpenAI model, or send them directly to OpenAI. You can also attach supplementary files, monitor jobs, and export results to Excel.

Structured PDF extraction Extract PDF data into workbook-style sheets.

Design the spreadsheet structure first: define ordered columns with questions and rules, then extract consistent rows from each article. Multiple worksheets can represent related types of information, supporting database-like organization and comparisons across studies.

Example: For a review of treatments, create separate worksheets for study characteristics, participant populations, interventions, and outcomes. A study might have one study-level row, several population or intervention rows, and multiple outcome rows, each linked back to its source article.

The Excel-like display lets you define columns and review extracted rows in a familiar grid. You can scan results alongside their source PDFs and parse statuses before exporting.

Reuse source files across workbooks, review parsed rows and errors, and export a worksheet or full workbook to Excel. The sheets provide a tabular structure; CEREBRO does not automatically create database relationships between them.

Text extraction Extract information from CSV or Excel records.

Use existing text in a CSV or Excel file, such as titles, abstracts, notes, or screening records. Choose which columns to include in the prompt, and CEREBRO processes each row as an individual job.

Example: Screen a spreadsheet of article titles and abstracts against review criteria, or categorize comments and notes collected in a study.

The workbook-style display shows selected input columns alongside job statuses and result previews, making it easier to review and find records while processing continues.

This works well for large record sets and preserves the original columns alongside the extracted results. Use separate sheets to compare prompts or models and retry failed rows.

Projects Organize files, jobs, and results in separate workspaces.

Choose an extraction type when creating a project, then return to that project's matching dashboard from the Projects page.

Sort projects by name or date, archive and restore them, or delete a project and its stored data when it is no longer needed.
